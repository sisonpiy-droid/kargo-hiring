KARGO HIRING RUBRIC (v2)
Criteria and weights follow the reference dashboard. Each criterion is scored 1-5.
The previous rubric is kept in rubric-v1.txt.

==================================================
PRODUCT MANAGER (PM)
==================================================

Criterion 1: Ship, learn & kill in short cycles

What a strong candidate looks like:
The CV shows features shipped in short cycles and then judged on real usage: specific launches, the adoption or usage data that followed, and at least one feature the candidate killed, rolled back or redirected because the data said so. Shipping lists without any post-launch evidence score 2 at most.

Weight: 25%

Criterion 2: Unforced adoption with operational impact

What a strong candidate looks like:
The candidate built a tool, process or practice that other people adopted without being told to, such as a sheet, checklist or workflow that became a team or branch standard, and it changed how operations ran. Evidence must name who adopted it and what changed. Mandated rollouts do not count.

Weight: 25%

Criterion 3: Engineering trust & self-built rhythm

What a strong candidate looks like:
The candidate worked directly with engineers with no translation layer and set up the working rhythm themselves: sprint rituals, specs, API documentation, or discovery-to-sprint handoffs they created, ideally as the only PM or without a product manager above them.

Weight: 20%

Criterion 4: Ground-level operations immersion

What a strong candidate looks like:
The candidate has done hands-on logistics or operations work (warehouse, dispatch, carrier allocation, customs, 3PL, freight forwarding) or embedded with operations teams for a sustained period, and can name the concrete tasks and volumes. Observation from a distance, consulting, or interviews alone score 2 at most.

Weight: 30%

TOTAL: 100%


==================================================
SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER (SPM)
==================================================

Criterion 1: Ground-level operations depth

What a strong candidate looks like:
Years of hands-on operational work in logistics, freight, customs, ports or warehousing, ideally running a team or shift, with concrete responsibilities and volumes. The candidate's product decisions visibly draw on that operational knowledge.

Weight: 30%

Criterion 2: Autonomous calls in ambiguity

What a strong candidate looks like:
The candidate owned product decisions with no layer above them (sole PM, owner of an area) and made hard calls alone: killing features or projects based on data, absorbing sunk cost, or setting direction where nothing was defined. Evidence must show the decision and its outcome.

Weight: 25%

Criterion 3: Integration & platform judgment

What a strong candidate looks like:
The candidate led integrations or platform work (carrier, customs, ERP or TMS APIs, data pipelines) and shows judgment about what the data and systems can and cannot do, e.g. designing around known API or data limitations, with a concrete result.

Weight: 30%

Criterion 4: Cross-functional unblocking & standards

What a strong candidate looks like:
The candidate unblocked work across teams (several engineering squads, customers, operations) and left standards or practices that others adopted beyond their own team, such as a tracker, discovery practice or process that spread.

Weight: 15%

TOTAL: 100%


==================================================
SCORING INSTRUCTIONS
==================================================

1. Score every candidate on ALL PM criteria and ALL SPM criteria, each 1-5 (1 = no evidence, 3 = some real evidence, 5 = strong, specific, repeated evidence).
2. For each criterion, quote or closely paraphrase the CV evidence behind the score.
3. Match % = the weighted score for the role applied for, as a percentage of 5.
4. Risk flags, from the list below only, each add 30 risk points (capped at 100):
   - No operations exposure: no hands-on operations or logistics work at all.
   - Structure dependency: has only worked inside established PM structures (a manager above, defined roadmaps) with no evidence of operating alone.
   - Keyword-only claims: relies on titles, frameworks, certifications or buzzwords with no concrete outcomes.
   - No shipped outcomes: no evidence of anything shipped reaching real users.
5. Use only evidence present in the CV. Do not use name, email, phone, age, gender, college brand or years of experience.
